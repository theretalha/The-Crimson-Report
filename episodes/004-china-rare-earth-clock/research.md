# EP 004 research brief: China's rare earth pause and the Jan 10 clock

Compiled Nov 27, 2026 (cloud session, web search). Reuters, AP, CNBC, WSJ, FT, Bloomberg and NYT pages were not opened; facts come from search snippets and secondary outlets. `fact-check.md` lists every number with its source and status. Re-check anything marked UNVERIFIED before recording.

## The story in one paragraph
On Nov 9, 2026 China's commerce ministry extended its suspension of the newest rare earth, battery-material and processing-equipment export controls until Jan 10, 2027, a day before the pause was due to lapse. The pause comes from the Oct 30, 2025 Trump-Xi truce in Busan. It never covered the April 2025 licence rules, which still apply to seven medium and heavy rare earths and the magnets made from them. Nine days before the Chinese pause ends, on Jan 1, 2027, a US defense procurement rule takes effect that bars certain magnets made with Chinese material, yet the supply chain to replace them does not exist yet. The video explains the metals, the chokepoint, the licence system, what the customs data shows, the American deadline, and three possible endings.

## Why now (the catalysts, newest first)
- Nov 19-20, 2026: Trump and Xi met in Shenzhen (APEC). Output: a pledge to draft a "stability action plan". Beijing's readout did not mention rare earths; the Jan deadline was not settled in public (CNBC Nov 20; US News/Reuters analysis Nov 21).
- Nov 9, 2026: MOFCOM extended the suspension to Jan 10, 2027 (S&P Global; Benchmark Minerals; Global Trade Alert lists it as Announcement 2026/58).
- Oct 30, 2026: Platts assessed dysprosium oxide at $4,100/kg CIF North America (S&P, Nov 9).
- Q3 2026: Benchmark: ex-China dysprosium oxide prices up about 150% in the quarter; Europe pays about 16.3 times the China price.
- Sept 2026: Bessent said the truce conditions run to Jan 10 and he was unsure whether a bigger deal or another extension would follow (Korea Times, Sept 24).
- Jan 1, 2027: US DFARS magnet restriction starts (10 U.S.C. 4872; DFARS 225.7018 and clause 252.225-7052). Contractors reportedly asked for a second delay (Adamas Intelligence).

## Key facts used in the script
- 17 rare earth elements; China mined about 61% and processed about 91% of major rare earths in 2024 (IEA, quoted by S&P Global). Foreign Policy puts processing nearer 85%, so say "about".
- Heavy rare earth separation: one industry estimate says China near 99% (Rare Earth Exchanges, trade press). Treat as an estimate.
- Lynas (Australia) separated dysprosium in May 2025 and terbium in June 2025 at Kuantan, Malaysia; about 8 tonnes of Dy and Tb in Q1 2026 against about 14 tonnes a month China exported to Japan in 2024 (Fastmarkets).
- F-35 figure: about 417 kg of rare earth material per jet is widely quoted, but Adamas Intelligence traces it to a single 2012 Pentagon study never released.
- EV magnets: one data review averages about 1.5 kg of magnet per electric car with a range of 0 to 4 kg; USGS-based estimates of neodymium per motor are much lower (about 200 g Nd and 30 g Dy). The script uses the 1.5 kg figure and labels it "one data review".
- CSIS "One Year After Busan": in the ten months after the deal, total exports of covered materials fell 2.5%, but combined shipments to the US and Japan fell 58%. CSIS "Rare Earth Export Restrictions One Year Later": magnet exports rose 13% after the easing while US imports fell 11% that month and Europe jumped 60%.
- Global Trade Alert argues China kept its word overall (no metal shipped below pre-Busan norms in volume).
- Price chain: S&P September average $3,500/kg Dy oxide CIF Europe; Platts $4,100/kg CIF North America on Oct 30; Benchmark +150% in Q3 and about 16.3 times the China price.
- MP Materials: DoD $400M preferred stock, 10-year $110/kg NdPr floor (one analysis puts China NdPr under $60/kg then), 10X magnet facility commissioning 2028 for about 10,000 tonnes a year.
- Lynas: new heavy separation facility in Malaysia (up to 5,000 tpa of heavy feedstock), first heavy products expected within two years.

## Both sides (balance)
- Beijing: export controls are a normal national security and non-proliferation tool; MOFCOM said the Oct 2025 scope was "limited" and promised licence facilitation; the Japan measures target military end users.
- Washington and Tokyo: the licence system is applied selectively and works as pressure; Japan called the Jan 2026 curbs "unacceptable".
- Independent: Global Trade Alert (China kept its word) versus CSIS (flows to the US and Japan fell 58%). Analysts also stress that building a mine-to-magnet chain outside China takes years.
